## What Is the Qibla?

The qibla is the direction Muslims face when performing salah — toward the Kaaba in the Masjid al-Haram in Mecca, Saudi Arabia. Facing the qibla is a condition for the validity of the prayer for those who are able to determine it, so finding the correct direction matters wherever you are in the world.

From most locations, the qibla is not simply "east." From North America it points northeast, from Europe southeast, and from parts of Asia west or northwest. The exact bearing is calculated as the shortest great-circle path from your coordinates to Mecca (21.4225 N, 39.8262 E).

## How to Find the Qibla With Your Phone

Modern phones make finding the qibla straightforward. A qibla compass app combines two things:

1. **Your location** — from GPS — to compute the true qibla bearing for exactly where you stand.
2. **Your phone's magnetometer** — the built-in compass sensor — to show which way you are currently facing.

The app then displays the difference: rotate until the indicator reads zero, and you are facing the qibla. Some apps show this as a compass dial with a marker, others as a panoramic view that pans as you turn.

To use a qibla finder:

1. Stand away from large metal objects and electronics.
2. Hold the phone flat or upright, as the app instructs.
3. Allow location access so the bearing is computed for your position.
4. Slowly rotate until the app shows you are aligned with the qibla direction.

## Why Phone Compasses Can Be Slightly Off

Phone compasses are convenient but imperfect. Here is why readings sometimes drift:

- **Magnetic interference.** Speakers, phone cases with magnets, reinforced concrete, cars, and even nearby electronics distort the magnetic field the sensor reads. This is the most common cause of a wrong reading.
- **Uncalibrated sensor.** If the compass has not been calibrated recently, it can be off by several degrees. The classic fix is the figure-eight motion: wave the phone in a figure-eight pattern a few times to recalibrate.
- **Magnetic vs. true north.** Phone compasses read magnetic north, but the qibla bearing is measured from true north. The difference between the two — magnetic declination — varies by location and changes slowly over time. Good qibla apps correct for declination automatically.
- **Sensor noise.** Cheap or aging sensors jitter. A well-built app smooths the heading so the display does not jump around.

If two different apps disagree by a degree or two, that is normal sensor variation. If they disagree by much more, magnetic interference or a missing declination correction is usually the cause.

## Tips for an Accurate Qibla Reading

- **Calibrate first.** Do the figure-eight motion before you start, especially if you have not used the compass in a while.
- **Step away from metal.** Move a few meters from cars, fridges, radiators, and structural steel.
- **Remove magnetic accessories.** Magnetic phone mounts, wallet cases, and pop sockets with metal can all pull the reading off.
- **Compare with a known reference.** If you know the qibla direction in your home or local mosque, sanity-check the app against it.
- **Give it a moment.** Let the reading settle for a few seconds rather than trusting the first number that appears.
- **When in doubt, ask.** At a mosque or with knowledgeable locals, a quick question beats a doubtful compass.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which direction is the qibla from my location?

It depends on where you are. From North America the qibla is generally northeast, from Europe southeast, from South Asia west. A qibla finder app computes the exact bearing from your GPS coordinates.

### Why does my phone compass point the wrong way indoors?

Buildings contain steel, wiring, and electronics that distort the magnetic field. Step outside or away from large metal objects and recalibrate with a figure-eight motion.

### Do I need internet to find the qibla?

You need GPS for your location, which works without internet on most phones. The bearing calculation itself is simple math and can be done offline once your coordinates are known.

### How accurate are phone qibla finders?

Typically within a few degrees when calibrated and used away from interference — more than adequate for prayer. For exact precision, such as orienting a new mosque, professional surveying is used.
