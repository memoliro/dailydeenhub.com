## What Is Tasbih and Dhikr?

**Dhikr** means the remembrance of Allah — repeating short phrases of praise, glorification, and supplication. **Tasbih** is the act of counting those repetitions, traditionally done on a string of prayer beads (also called tasbih or misbaha), and today often on a digital tasbih counter.

Dhikr is one of the simplest and most encouraged forms of worship. It needs no special time, place, or ablution to begin — it can be done while walking, waiting, or resting. The Quran says, "So remember Me; I will remember you" (2:152), and the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) taught specific phrases and counts for daily remembrance.

## The Most Common Dhikr Phrases

These are the phrases most Muslims repeat in daily dhikr, with their meanings:

- **Subhanallah** — "Glory be to Allah." Declaring Allah free of any imperfection.
- **Alhamdulillah** — "All praise is due to Allah." Expressing gratitude for every blessing.
- **Allahu Akbar** — "Allah is the Greatest." Affirming that Allah is greater than everything.
- **La ilaha illallah** — "There is no god but Allah." The core declaration of faith; the Prophet (peace be upon him) called it the best dhikr.
- **Astaghfirullah** — "I seek Allah's forgiveness." Said to turn back to Allah and seek pardon.
- **La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah** — "There is no power nor strength except with Allah." A phrase the Prophet (peace be upon him) described as a treasure of Paradise.

A beloved daily routine, taught by the Prophet (peace be upon him), is to say Subhanallah, Alhamdulillah, and Allahu Akbar 33 times each after every prayer, completing one hundred with "La ilaha illallah wahdahu la sharika lah, lahul-mulku wa lahul-hamd, wa huwa ala kulli shay'in qadir."

## Why 33 and 99?

The numbers 33 and 99 come directly from the Sunnah. The Prophet (peace be upon him) advised saying Subhanallah 33 times, Alhamdulillah 33 times, and Allahu Akbar 33 times after each obligatory prayer (Sahih Muslim). That is 99, completed to 100 with the declaration of faith.

Beyond the post-prayer routine, 100 is a common daily target — for example, saying "Subhanallahi wa bihamdihi" 100 times a day was described as wiping away sins even if they were like the foam of the sea (Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim). A counter simply helps you keep track without losing count.

## Digital Tasbih vs. Physical Prayer Beads

A traditional tasbih is a string of 33 or 99 beads, sometimes 100 or 1000. A digital tasbih counter serves the same purpose with a few practical advantages:

- **Always with you.** Your phone is already in your pocket; a bead string is easy to forget at home.
- **Exact counts and targets.** Set a target like 99 or 100 and the counter tracks your progress precisely, with a gentle signal when you reach it.
- **Multiple dhikr lists.** Switch between phrases, keep custom counts, and save your own adhkar — things a single bead string cannot do.
- **Silent mode.** A digital counter can vibrate instead of clicking, useful in quiet places.

Physical beads have their own charm — the tactile rhythm, no battery, no screen. Many people use both: beads at home, a digital counter on the go. Neither is more virtuous; what matters is the remembrance itself.

## Building a Daily Dhikr Habit

- **Attach dhikr to existing moments.** After each prayer is the Sunnah anchor; commuting, waiting rooms, and walks are natural extras.
- **Start small.** One set of 33 after one prayer beats an ambitious plan you abandon.
- **Use a target.** A visible count like 99 gives the session a finish line and a small sense of completion.
- **Learn the meanings.** Knowing what "Alhamdulillah" means transforms repetition into reflection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I say on a tasbih?

The most common are Subhanallah, Alhamdulillah, and Allahu Akbar — 33 times each. You can also count La ilaha illallah, Astaghfirullah, or any authentic supplication.

### How many beads are on a tasbih?

Most have 33 or 99 beads, matching the Sunnah counts after prayer. Longer ones of 100, 500, or 1000 beads exist for extended dhikr sessions.

### Is a digital tasbih counter permissible?

Yes. Scholars consider counting dhikr on fingers, beads, or a digital counter all permissible — they are simply tools for keeping count. The Prophet (peace be upon him) himself encouraged counting dhikr.

### What is the best dhikr to recite daily?

"La ilaha illallah" was described by the Prophet (peace be upon him) as the best dhikr. The trio of Subhanallah, Alhamdulillah, and Allahu Akbar 33 times each after prayer is the most established daily routine.
